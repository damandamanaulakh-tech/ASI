The Mirror Structure of Odd Primestested against four established approaches to RHRipudaman SinghMay 2026AbstractThis paper takes the mirror-structure observation that every odd prime N has the form N = k + 1 + k, with the integer 1 as mirror axis, and tests it against four established approaches to the Riemann Hypothesis: (1) the Hilbert-Pólya operator programme (Yakaboylu 2024, LeClair 2025); (2) the original V3 paper construction; (3) numerical comparison against the first 20 Riemann zeros; (4) the author's own URR verification sequence.The honest result is: the structural observation is internally consistent and matches the symmetry of Riemann's functional equation, but it does not by itself close the gap to any of the four established formal approaches. The numerical test shows the framework has no statistical predictive power over and above random chance. The paper records this honestly so the writer can read it again later with rested eyes.1. The structural observationEvery odd prime N can be written N = k + 1 + k, where k = (N-1)/2 and the integer 1 is the mirror axis. The two halves k are equal. The prime is the union of an origin (1) and its two mirrored halves.Riemann's critical line carries the same shape: s = 1/2 + it has mirror 1 - s = 1/2 - it. The rational 1/2 is the mirror axis, and the two halves +it and -it are equal in magnitude.The shape is identical. Only the scale of the axis differs (1 for integer primes, 1/2 for the complex zeros).2. Test against the Hilbert-Pólya operator programmeYakaboylu (2024) constructs a candidate Hamiltonian H = -i(x d/dx + 1/2) with the involution boundary condition psi(x) = psi(1/x). The condition makes 1 (the integer) the mirror point in x-space and 1/2 (the rational) the mirror point in s-space via the Mellin transform.This matches our observation exactly. Yakaboylu's involution at x = 1 is structurally the same mirror-around-1 that holds for odd primes in N = k + 1 + k. His conclusion that eigenvalues land at Re(s) = 1/2 is structurally the same mirror-around-1/2.What this means for the present paper:• Our structural observation says the same thing Yakaboylu says, in elementary language.• Yakaboylu's construction is more formal but has the same gap our paper has: it does not prove the spectral determinant of his operator equals ξ(1/2 + iE).• LeClair (2025) reformulates RH as a Bethe-ansatz completeness condition, which is equivalent to the Euler product being controllable on Re(s) = 1/2. This is RH-equivalent, not a proof.Result: our framework lands in the same place as the published attempts. We have a structural reason; they have a more formal apparatus; nobody has the proof.3. Test against the V3 paper constructionThe earlier V3 paper used a 27-step sequence, a four-lock theorem, partition functions, and the Bost–Connes embedding. Each lock claimed to force eigenvalues onto Re(s) = 1/2.Our structural observation reduces to a single mirror principle. It does not need four locks; it gives one reason: the centre of the prime mirror (1) scales to the centre of the zero mirror (1/2).Comparison:• V3's locks 1, 2, 4 (deficiency indices, PT-symmetry, positivity of spectral measure) all reduce to: self-adjoint implies real eigenvalues. Our framework agrees, but adds the structural reason WHY a self-adjoint operator with the right boundary condition produces eigenvalues at 1/2 — because the boundary mirror is the integer 1, which scales to 1/2 in s-space.• V3's lock 3 (S-matrix unitarity) is circular as written; our framework does not need it.• V3's Bost-Connes invocation is also unnecessary in our framework: we do not need the partition function structure. The mirror structure of primes around 1 is the load-bearing claim.Result: our framework is simpler than V3 but reaches the same end-point: a structural argument, not a formal proof.4.
