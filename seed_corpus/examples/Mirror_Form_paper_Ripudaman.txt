The Mirror Form k + 1 + kAn alternative parametrization for odd primes and their structuresRipudaman SinghMay 2026AbstractThis paper introduces a single notational substitution: every odd integer n can be written uniquely as n = k + 1 + k, where k = (n − 1)/2. The form has an integer 1 as its centre and two equal half-blocks k on either side. We apply this notation to six standard prime structures — primality testing, Goldbach pairs, prime gaps, Cunningham chains, safe primes, and twin primes — and report exactly what we tested, exactly what we found, and exactly what the notation does and does not change. No claim is made beyond what was verified in the tested ranges. No claim is made about the Riemann Hypothesis or any other open problem.1. The notationDefine, for every odd integer n ≥ 3:n = k + 1 + k,   where k = (n − 1) / 2The integer 1 is the centre. The two blocks k are equal. The notation is a bijection between odd integers n ≥ 3 and positive integers k ≥ 1.This is a substitution, not a discovery. Every odd integer satisfies it. The substitution is offered here as a way of writing prime structures so that their mirror character around 1 becomes visible at every step.2. MethodFor each of the six structures below we did three things:(a) Restated the standard definition in the mirror notation.(b) Ran code to test the restatement against the standard definition on a specific numerical range.(c) Recorded both what was confirmed in the tested range and the honest limits of the notation.All numerical work was done in Python with the sympy library for prime testing. Source code is available on request.3. ResultsThe full table of results follows. Each row covers one structure.
