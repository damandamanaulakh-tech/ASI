The Mirror Structure of Odd Primesand its parallel with the critical line of ζ(s)Ripudaman SinghElectrical Engineer  ·  GM ProjectsMay 2026AbstractEvery odd prime N has the structural form N = k + 1 + k, where the two halves k are equal and the integer 1 serves as the mirror axis. The Riemann critical line has the same structural form: s = ½ + it and 1 − s = ½ − it, where the two halves +it and −it are equal in magnitude and ½ serves as the mirror axis. This paper records the parallel between these two mirror structures — one for primes around the integer 1, the other for the nontrivial zeros of ζ around the rational ½ — as a structural argument that the critical line is the natural location for the zeros.1. The structural form of odd primesA prime is defined the standard way: a positive integer greater than 1 that is divisible only by 1 and itself. This definition is the test for primality. Nothing in this paper replaces or modifies that definition.This paper concerns the structural form of a prime once primality has been established. For every odd prime N, there exists an integer k such that:N  =  k  +  1  +  kwhere k = (N − 1) / 2The two halves k are equal. The 1 in the middle is the mirror axis.
