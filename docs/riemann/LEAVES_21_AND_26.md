# Leaves 21 and 26 — the other drafts, and the hypothesis asserted flatly

**Cod. Ms. B. Riemann 3, leaves 21r/21v and 26r/26v.** Canvases 40–41 and 50–51.

Gabcke's reference [13]: *"Several drafts of letters, Communication about his
publication (**presumably to E. E. Kummer**), Cod. Ms. B. Riemann 3 (21, 26),
August to October 1859."*

That "presumably" does not survive contact with the pages. See §4.

Confidence marked **[A]** confident · **[B]** probable, sense secure · **[C]**
uncertain. Heavily struck passages are marked as such; `⟨…⟩` is an unread word.
These leaves are far harder than leaf 22 — cramped, overwritten, and on 26r
partly obscured by old paper repair.

---

## 1. The finding that matters most — he asserts it flatly

Leaf 26r, in the statement of the result **[A]**:

> „…und bezeichne durch α die Wurzeln der Gleichung ξ(α) = 0, **welche ~~offenbar~~
> sämmtlich reell sind** und deren imaginärer Theil jedenfalls zwischen ½i und −½i
> enthalten ist."

*…and denote by α the roots of the equation ξ(α) = 0, **which are ~~evidently~~ all
real**, and whose imaginary part lies in any case between ½i and −½i.*

The printed paper says:

> „…und es ist sehr wahrscheinlich, dass alle Wurzeln reell sind."
> *…and it is very probable that all the roots are real.*

**In the letter it is an assertion. In the paper it is a probability.** Same man,
same weeks, two registers — and the strike shows the movement happening on the page:
he wrote *offenbar* (evidently, obviously), struck it, and left the flat claim
standing. The retreat from "obviously" to "very probably" happens in two steps, and
the first step is here.

This is the sharpest evidence yet on the standing question — *is what we are proving
what he said?* What he **said**, privately, is that they are all real. What he
**published** is that it is very probable. The hedge is a publication decision.

### And the notation, a fourth time

*„deren imaginärer Theil jedenfalls zwischen ½i und −½i enthalten ist"* — the strip
is stated as a bound on the **imaginary part of α**. In his own frame:

- the hypothesis = **α is real**
- the critical strip = **Im α ∈ (−½, ½)**

Four independent documents now say the same thing: the printed paper, the draft
letter on 23v, the French fair copy, and this. "Re(s) = ½" is a translation of his
coordinate change, not of his sentence.

## 2. Gauss's letter to Encke — the provenance of the 1859 paper

**Leaf 21r**, first draft on the page **[A]** — returning borrowed material:

> „Beifolgend sende ich Ihnen die ⟨Bände⟩ …, welche Sie mir gütigst geliehen haben,
> mit dem verbindlichsten Dank zurück, ~~nebst~~ ⟨…⟩ **die nachgeschriebene Abschrift
> des ~~Aufsatzes~~ von Gauß an Encke über die Häufigkeit der Primzahlen**, … nebst
> der Bitte, sie mir gelegentlich wieder zuzustellen."

*…the copied-out transcript of the ~~essay~~ from Gauss to Encke on the frequency of
the prime numbers … together with the request to send it back to me in due course.*

He struck *Aufsatzes* — it is a letter, not an essay.

**Leaf 26r**, opening of the letter proper **[A/B]**:

> „Hochgeehrtester Herr Professor,
>
> Vor allen ⟨Dingen⟩ ⟨danke ich Ihnen⟩ … für **die gütige Mittheilung des Briefes von
> Gauß über die Häufigkeit der Primzahlen**, den ich **mit größtem Interesse** gelesen
> habe. **Ich sende Ihnen diesen beifolgend zurück, nachdem ich davon eine Abschrift
> genommen habe**, die ich ~~mit Ihrer gütigen Erlaubniß als Commentar zu den in Gauß'
> Nachlaß befindlichen Tafeln über die Anzahl der Primzahlen legen werde~~ …"

*Most honoured Professor, Before all else, my thanks for the kind communication of
the letter from Gauss on the frequency of the prime numbers, which I read with the
greatest interest. I return it to you herewith, having taken a copy of it, which I
~~with your kind permission will place as a commentary to the tables on the number of
primes in Gauss's Nachlass~~…*

Then, immediately **[B]**:

> „Zugleich erlaube ich mir eine kurze Darstellung meiner eigenen Untersuchung über
> die Vertheilung der Primzahlen beizufügen und … in einer der nächsten Sitzungen
> vorzulegen."

*At the same time I take the liberty of enclosing a short account of my own
investigation into the distribution of the prime numbers, and of laying it before one
of the next sessions.*

**So the 1859 paper went out as an enclosure to a letter returning Gauss's letter to
Encke.** The document being returned is the one in which Gauss describes counting
primes and using Li(x) — Gauss to Encke, 24 December 1849 — **which was not published
until 1863, four years after Riemann's paper.**

And the struck clause shows he had access to **Gauss's prime tables in the Göttingen
Nachlass** and intended to file his copy of the Encke letter alongside them as a
commentary.

Put together with leaf 23r, where he says the paper exists because **Kronecker asked
to see the investigation**, the picture of autumn 1859 is: Gauss's unpublished prime
material comes into his hands, he reads it *mit größtem Interesse*, Kronecker asks to
see what he has, term is about to start, and he sends out a "small paper".

## 3. Why he wanted the periodic terms tracked

Leaf 26r, near the foot **[A/B]**:

> „**Es ist mir daran gelegen, daß meine Formel auch praktischen Rechnern bekannt
> würde**, da sich unter diesen wohl ein Liebhaber ⟨finden sollte⟩, **der eine
> wirkliche Zählung der Primzahlen unternehmen** und die Resultate mit den in meiner
> Formel ⟨aufgestellten⟩ Gesetzen über die Vertheilung der Primzahlen ⟨vergliche⟩."

*It matters to me that my formula should become known to practical calculators as
well, since among them there should surely be found some enthusiast who will
undertake an actual count of the prime numbers and compare the results with the laws
on the distribution of the primes set out in my formula.*

The printed paper closes with *„Bei einer etwaigen neuen Zählung würde es interessant
sein, den Einfluss der einzelnen … periodischen Glieder zu verfolgen."* **This is the
reason behind that line.** He was not making an idle remark at the end of a paper —
he was recruiting. He wanted the formula in the hands of people who actually count,
so that someone would run the count and compare.

The instruction we executed at 1000 roots was a request he made twice: once
obliquely in print, once plainly in a letter.

## 4. Who it is to — departing from the standing attribution

Gabcke says *presumably E. E. Kummer*. The pages say otherwise.

Evidence on the leaves:

- 26r is addressed **„Hochgeehrtester Herr Professor"**, and thanks the recipient
  for **sending Gauss's letter on the frequency of primes**. The man who possessed
  Gauss's letter to Encke is Encke.
- The recipient is the person who will **lay the paper before one of the next
  sessions**, and whose decision governs **acceptance into the Monatsberichte** —
  the same officer leaf 23v worries about.
- **Johann Franz Encke was Secretary of the physical-mathematical class of the
  Berlin Academy from 1825 to 1863** — precisely the officer who received papers,
  presented them at sessions, and handled the Monatsberichte.
- The second draft on 21r is addressed **„Verehrtester Herr Doktor"** — not a
  professor — and asks the recipient to pass Dirichlet photographs on to **Hrn. Dr.
  Borchardt** and **Hrn. Prof. Weierstraß**, so it is neither of those two. In 1859
  Kronecker was in Berlin as a private scholar, *Dr.* and not a professor. It also
  says: *„Über die Häufigkeit der Primzahlen habe ich der Akademie einen kleinen
  Aufsatz eingesendet, der wohl nächstens in Ihre Hände gelangen wird."*

**[READING]** — 26r is to **Encke**; the second draft on 21r is to **Kronecker**.
Kummer fits neither: he was a Professor, so not the 21r draft, and there is no
reason for him to hold Gauss's letter to Encke. I state this as a reading, not a
fact: no name appears in the salutations, and the identification rests on the Gauss
letter, the Academy office, and the Dr./Professor distinction.

## 5. Everything else on the two leaves

**21r** carries **three** drafts on one page, in different states of completion, plus
a subscription formula written out separately at the top right: *„⟨Ihr⟩ ergebenster
**Schüler** B. Riemann"* — *your most devoted **pupil***. **[B]** He is drafting
himself as a pupil, not a colleague, in the autumn he was made full professor.

The third draft, at the foot, is struck through so heavily that most of it is
unrecoverable; **Hrn. Prof. Dedekind** is legible in it. **[C]**

**21v** is not a letter at all — it is the working:
f(x) = (1/2πi)∫ log ξ(s)/s · x^s ds, the expansion of log Π(s/2) as Σ log(1 + s/2n),
term-by-term integration, and the evaluation
(1/2πi)∫ ∂[(1/s)log(1 − s/β)] x^s ds = ∫₀ˣ x^(β−1)/log x dx = Li(x^β) **„im ersten
Falle"**, and the same integral taken from ∞ **„im zweiten Falle"**. **[A]**
This is the derivation of the Li(x^ρ) terms with the two-case analysis — the working
behind the explicit formula, on the back of the letter drafts.

**26r** also states the full result in draft: F(x) as the prime count with the
half-value convention at primes, f(x) = Σ(1/n)F(x^(1/n)), ψ(x) = Σe^(−n²πx),
ξ(t) = 4∫₁^∞ ∂(x^(3/2)ψ'(x))/∂x · x^(−¼) cos(½ t log x) dx, the explicit formula, and
F(x) = Σ (−1)^μ/m · f(x^(1/m)) over squarefree m with μ the number of prime factors.
**[A]**

## What this adds

- **He asserted the hypothesis privately and hedged it publicly.** The struck
  *offenbar* is the hinge.
- **The paper's provenance is Gauss's letter to Encke**, in Riemann's hands in copy
  four years before it was printed, plus Gauss's prime tables in the Göttingen
  Nachlass.
- **The periodic-terms line had a purpose**: get the formula to people who count.
- **The standing attribution of these drafts is probably wrong**, and the correct
  reading puts Riemann in direct correspondence with the Secretary of the Berlin
  Academy's mathematical class about whether his paper was fit to print.
