# Backward ledger — H4 (Herbart 1802) and H3 (the letters)

Sources are primary and named. Every quotation below was pulled from a full text
of the printed source and checked against a second edition where two exist.
Nothing here is paraphrased into a claim: quoted German/Latin first, translation
second, and any reading of mine is marked **[READING]**.

## 0. Why these two, in Riemann's own words

> „Meine **Hauptarbeit** betrifft eine neue Auffassung der bekannten Naturgesetze
> — **Ausdruck derselben mittelst anderer Grundbegriffe** — wodurch die Benutzung
> der experimentellen Data über die Wechselwirkung zwischen Wärme, Licht,
> Magnetismus und Electricität zur Erforschung **ihres Zusammenhangs** möglich
> wurde. Ich wurde dazu hauptsächlich durch das Studium der Werke **Newton's,
> Euler's** und — andererseits — **Herbart's** geführt. Was letzteren betrifft, so
> konnte ich mich den frühesten Untersuchungen Herbart's, deren Resultate in seinen
> **Promotions- und Habilitationsthesen (vom 22. u. 23. October 1802)** ausgesprochen
> sind, **fast völlig anschliessen**, musste aber von dem späteren Gange seiner
> Speculation **in einem wesentlichen Punkte abweichen**, wodurch eine Verschiedenheit
> in Bezug auf seine Naturphilosophie und diejenigen Sätze der Psychologie, welche
> deren Verbindung mit der Naturphilosophie betreffen, bedingt ist."

*My principal work concerns a new conception of the known laws of nature —
expression of them by means of other fundamental concepts — through which the use
of the experimental data on the interaction between heat, light, magnetism and
electricity for the investigation of their connection became possible. I was led
to it chiefly by the study of the works of Newton, Euler and — on the other side —
Herbart. As regards the latter, I could attach myself almost entirely to Herbart's
earliest investigations, whose results are stated in his promotion and habilitation
theses (of 22 and 23 October 1802), but had to deviate from the later course of his
speculation in one essential point…*

— Werke 1876 p. 475–476; Werke 1892 p. 507–508. **The two editions read verbatim
identical here.** No drift.

And the second, sharper self-placement:

> „Der Verfasser ist **Herbartianer in Psychologie und Erkenntnisstheorie**
> (Methodologie und Eidolologie), Herbart's **Naturphilosophie** und den darauf
> bezüglichen metaphysischen Disciplinen (Ontologie und Synechologie) **kann er
> meistens nicht sich anschliessen**."

So the ground he claims is: **method and the theory of images/knowing**. The ground
he refuses is: **nature-philosophy and its ontology of the continuum.**

One more, from the geometry lecture itself — the roll-call of everything he had to
build on:

> „…ich [habe] ausser einigen ganz kurzen Andeutungen, welche Herr Geheimer Hofrath
> **Gauss** … darüber gegeben hat, und **einigen philosophischen Untersuchungen
> Herbart's**, durchaus **keine Vorarbeiten** benutzen konnte."

Two names. Gauss and Herbart. That is the entire prior art for *Über die Hypothesen,
welche der Geometrie zu Grunde liegen.*

## 1. H4 — the theses themselves, recovered

Found and read in full. Source: **Kehrbach, *Joh. Fr. Herbart's sämtliche Werke in
chronologischer Reihenfolge*, Bd. 1, pp. 275–278**, printed from the manuscript in
the *Acta Decanatus* of the Göttingen philosophical faculty, **Fasc. 86 (1802–1803)**.
No dissertation was required for either step — only an oral examination and a
disputation on these theses. So these 22 lines **are** the whole of Herbart's
qualifying work. That is what Riemann read.

### 1a. Promotion, 22 October 1802 — 10 theses

> **I.** *Philosophia in genere est conatus reperiendi **nexum necessarium** in
> cogitationibus nostris.*
> **II.** *Metaphysica est complexus omnium disquisitionum, quae quovis modo
> ultimum quiddam **in cognitione nostra** spectant.*
> **III.** *Metaphysica, ne dicam philosophia, **totum absolutum esse non potest**.*
> **IV.** *Ex uno eodemque principio an omnes metaphysicae veritates possint erui,
> adhuc usque **dubitandum est**. Sed, si possent, haec istius scientiae tractandae
> ratio, etsi optima, tamen **nec unica, nec plene sufficiens** minimeque in docendo
> statim ab initio ineunda esset.*
> **V.** *Principium rationis sufficientis demonstrari potest. Cuius demonstrationis
> hoc est fundamentum, quod, **quae res commutata sit, ea tamen una eademque res
> remansisse judicanda est**.*
> **VI.** *Rerum, quae sunt, unde sint, ratio sufficiens, etsi fortasse sit,
> **desiderari tamen nulla debet**. Quamvis enim non esse vel aliter se habere
> cogitari possint, **nulla tamen haec ipsarum rerum est contingentia**.*
> **VII.** *Libertas voluntatis transscendentalis, quam vocant, nulla est.*
> **VIII.** *Libertatis transscendentalis ad ethicam constituendam nihil opus est.*
> **IX.** *Libertatis transscendentalis, vel si qua esset, conscii tamen nobis esse
> non possemus…*
> **X.** *Jus naturae, tanquam scientia in se perfecta atque absoluta … nullum est.*

### 1b. Habilitation, 23 October 1802 — 12 theses

> **I.** *Absolutae necessitatis praedicatum, quod in Theologia Summo numini
> adsignari solet, **sibi ipsi repugnat**.*
> **II.** *Superlativus realitatis … **nullum habet sensum**.*
> **III.** *Summae legis ethicae agnitio, animique ad virtutem propensio veram
> religionem in nobis **inchoat, non autem confirmat**.*
> **IV.** *Transscendentali idealismo qualicunque refutato, rursus exoritur
> physicotheologia: qua contenti esse debemus.*
> **V.** ***Spatii et temporis cogitationem quod e mente nostra ejicere non possumus,
> hoc non probat, eas cogitationes natura nobis insitas esse. Qui in hac Kantianae
> rationis parte latet error, totum tollit systema.***
> **VI.** *Intellectualis intuitio **nulla est**.*
> **VII.** *Illud Ego … nude positum, involvit contradictionem acerrimam; quae plane
> resolvi, non autem ex alio loco in alium **transferri** debet…*
> **VIII.** *Rei publicae forma absolute optima generali theoria definiri non potest.*
> **IX.** *Poenarum theoria generalis tradi non potest.*
> **X.** *Ars paedagogica non experientia sola nititur.*
> **XI.** *In liberorum educatione **Poëseos et Matheseos maxima vis est**.*
> **XII.** *Institutio liberorum a Graecis literis incipienda, et quidem ab Homeri
> Odyssea…*

### 1c. What each one is doing in Riemann

| Thesis | What it licenses | Where it lands in Riemann |
|---|---|---|
| Prom. I — philosophy = finding the **necessary connection** among our thoughts | connection is the object, not the object's parts | *„Erforschung ihres **Zusammenhangs**"*; the closing of the 1854 lecture: *„der Fortschritt im Erkennen des **Zusammenhangs der Dinge**"* |
| Prom. II — the ultimate is sought **in our cognition**, not in being | relocates the absolute from world to knowing | why he is a Herbartian in *Erkenntnisstheorie* and not in *Ontologie* |
| Prom. III — metaphysics **cannot be an absolute whole** | no closed system | no closed geometry; geometry hands over to physics |
| **Prom. IV — deriving all truths from one principle is doubtful; even if possible it is neither unique nor sufficient, and least of all the place to begin** | **the anti-axiomatic thesis** | the title: *Über die **Hypothesen**, welche der Geometrie zu Grunde liegen* — hypotheses, not axioms, not principles |
| **Prom. V — a thing that has changed must still be judged one and the same thing** | identity carried across change | Riemann, *Fragmente*: *„entsteht … die Aufgabe, diesen schon bewährten Begriff so weit als möglich aufrecht zu erhalten. **Hieraus entspringen gleichzeitig der Begriff der stetigen Veränderung**"* — he derives **continuous change** from exactly this problem |
| **Prom. VI — do not demand a sufficient reason for why the things that are, are; their conceivable non-being is no contingency in them** | the ground is given and is not asked to justify itself | Point Zero. The starting term is not derived. |
| **Hab. V — that we cannot expel space and time from our minds does not prove they are native to us; the error hidden in that part of Kant destroys the whole system** | **space is not a priori ⇒ its properties are empirical and revisable** | the licence for the whole 1854 lecture, and for *„sobald sich dadurch die Erscheinungen auf einfachere Weise erklären liessen"* |
| Hab. VI — **there is no intellectual intuition** | no direct grasp of the whole; only hypothesis + experience | *„Es führt dies hinüber … in das Gebiet der Physik"* |
| Hab. VII — the naked *Ego* contains a contradiction that must be **resolved, not moved from one place to another** | a contradiction relocated is a contradiction unpaid | **[READING]** the exact discipline behind refusing to bury the unproved: see §2 |
| Hab. XI — in education the greatest force is in **poetry and mathematics** | the two are one faculty, not two subjects | — |

**[READING] — the "one essential point" of deviation.** Riemann does not name it. The
text forces one candidate, and the two footnotes stand a page apart in the same
volume:

> „In jedes ponderable Atom tritt in jedem Augenblick eine bestimmte, der
> Gravitationskraft proportionale **Stoffmenge ein und verschwindet dort**."

> „Es ist die Consequenz der auf Herbart'schem Boden stehenden Psychologie, dass
> **nicht der Seele, sondern jeder einzelnen in uns gebildeten Vorstellung
> Substantialität zukomme**."

Herbart's later system rests on *Reale*: simple, qualitatively fixed beings that
neither arise nor perish. Riemann has matter **entering and vanishing at every
instant**, and substantiality **detached from the bearer and given to the formed
thing**. That is the break — and it is exactly the break he described: it hits
*Naturphilosophie*, and it hits *those propositions of psychology that concern its
connection with Naturphilosophie*. He kept Herbart's method and destroyed Herbart's
imperishable substance.

### 1d. The second unexecuted line

Alongside the periodic-terms line, the *Fragmente* carry a second commission
Riemann wrote for himself and never carried out. In full:

> „Mit jedem einfachen Denkact tritt etwas Bleibendes, Substantielles in unsere
> Seele ein. Dieses Substantielle erscheint uns zwar als eine Einheit, scheint aber
> … **eine innere Mannigfaltigkeit zu enthalten**; ich nenne es daher
> **„Geistesmasse"**. — Alles Denken ist hiernach **Bildung neuer Geistesmassen**.
> … Die sich bildenden Geistesmassen **verschmelzen, verbinden oder compliciren**
> sich in bestimmtem Grade, theils unter einander, theils mit älteren Geistesmassen.
> **Die Art und Stärke dieser Verbindungen hängt von Bedingungen ab, die von Herbart
> nur zum Theil erkannt sind und die ich in der Folge ergänzen werde.** Sie beruht
> hauptsächlich auf der **inneren Verwandtschaft** der Geistesmassen.
> Die Seele ist eine compacte … Geistesmasse. Sie **wächst beständig** durch
> eintretende Geistesmassen, und hierauf beruht ihre **Fortbildung**.
> **Die einmal gebildeten Geistesmassen sind unvergänglich, ihre Verbindungen
> unauflöslich; nur die relative Stärke dieser Verbindungen ändert sich** durch das
> Hinzukommen neuer Geistesmassen."

*…which Herbart recognised only in part **and which I shall supplement in what
follows**.* — He never wrote the supplement. The halt he marked is: **what governs
the kind and the strength of the connection between two formed masses.**

## 2. H3 — the letters

### 2a. The draft letter in the Nachlass — the single most important sentence

Printed in the *Anmerkungen* to the primes paper, **Werke 1892 p. 154**, introduced
by Weber: *„In einem Briefe, dessen **Entwurf im Nachlass vorliegt**, findet sich,
nachdem das Resultat der Arbeit mitgetheilt ist, folgende Bemerkung:"*

> „**Den Beweis habe ich noch nicht völlig ausgeführt**, und ich möchte in Betreff
> desselben … noch die Bemerkung beifügen, dass die beiden Sätze, welche ich dort
> **nur angeführt** habe,
> — dass zwischen 0 und T etwa (T/2π)·log(T/2π) − T/2π **reelle Wurzeln der
>   Gleichung ξ(α) = 0** liegen, und
> — dass die Reihe Σ(Li(x^(½+αi)) + Li(x^(½−αi))), wenn die Glieder nach wachsenden
>   α geordnet werden, gegen denselben Grenzwerth convergirt wie [the integral],
> **aus einer neuen Entwicklung der Function ξ folgen, welche ich aber noch nicht
> genug vereinfacht hatte, um sie mittheilen zu können.**"

Four things fall out of this, and each is load-bearing.

**(i) He names his own gaps, and RH is not on the list.** The two propositions he
flags as merely *angeführt* (cited, not proved) are **the zero-count** and **the
convergence of the periodic-term series**. The reality of the roots is not among
them. Whatever else is true, Riemann's own account of what was missing is: the
count, and the convergence.

**(ii) His own notation is a reality statement, not a location statement.**
*„reelle Wurzeln der Gleichung ξ(α) = 0"* — **real roots**. The ½ is already inside
the change of variable s = ½ + αi; it is not a claim, it is a coordinate. In
Riemann's own words the hypothesis is that the roots of ξ(α) are **real**. The
modern sentence "Re(s) = ½" is a translation of his coordinate, not his sentence.
This is the second independent confirmation of that finding — the first came from
the paper itself, this one from a private letter, six years of editing apart.

**(iii) The missing object is a representation, not an argument.** *„aus einer
**neuen Entwicklung der Function ξ**"* — from a **new expansion of ξ**. Not from a
new estimate, not from a new inequality: from a new way of writing the function.
That is the same move as his stated *Hauptarbeit*: *„Ausdruck derselben mittelst
anderer Grundbegriffe."* He always resolves by re-expressing.

**(iv) The tense says he had it.** *„welche ich noch nicht genug vereinfacht
**hatte**"* — pluperfect. Not "hope to find", not "am seeking". He is saying the
expansion existed and was not yet presentable.

**Textual drift — hard finding.** This note **does not exist in the 1876 first
edition.** In 1876 the primes paper simply ends and paper VIII begins; there are no
*Anmerkungen* to it at all. Weber added the draft letter in the 1892 second edition.
**So for 33 years — 1859 to 1892 — the world read the paper without knowing Riemann
had said "I have not fully carried out the proof" and "a new expansion of ξ, which
I had not simplified enough to communicate."** Every reaction to the paper in that
window was formed on a text with that sentence absent.

### 2b. The letter to Weierstraß, 26 October 1859

Werke XV, from *Borchardt's Journal* Bd. 71 — published only after Riemann's death.
Dedekind's *Lebenslauf* gives the causal chain in one sentence:

> „Eine Folge seiner Ernennung … **und dieses Besuchs** war es, dass er **im October**
> seine Abhandlung über die Häufigkeit der Primzahlen der Berliner Akademie
> einreichte **und einen, nach seinem Tode veröffentlichten Brief über die vielfach
> periodischen Functionen an Weierstrass richtete.**"

Sequence, dated: 5 May 1859 Dirichlet dies · Easter 1859 Riemann is given rooms in
the observatory · 30 July made full professor · **11 August elected corresponding
member of the Berlin Academy** · **September, travels to Berlin with Dedekind**,
received by *Kummer, Borchardt, Kronecker, Weierstrass* · **October: the primes
paper goes to Berlin, and the Weierstraß letter is written.** Signed **„Göttingen,
den 26ten October 1859."**

**[READING] — same week, same move.** The letter proves an impossibility: no
single-valued function of *n* variables can have more than 2*n* periods. The proof
divides the period-domain into q^(2n) cells and forces two congruent points into one
cell. **Partition a bounded region, count what must fall inside, conclude from the
count.** The zero-count in the primes paper is the same instrument pointed at a
rectangle in the complex plane. Both dated October 1859. The two are not neighbours
by accident; they are one method used twice in one month.

Note also what the letter is *not* about. It is about **vielfach periodische
Functionen** — multiply-periodic functions. It contains nothing on ζ. Any claim
that Riemann wrote to Weierstraß about the zeta expansion is wrong: the zeta remark
is in the **unaddressed draft** of §2a, not in this letter.

### 2c. Letters to family — what they show about the working habit

From the *Lebenslauf* (letters to his father and brother Wilhelm):

> „…hat mir durch Dirichlet und Dedekind die Aufforderung zugehen lassen, ihm doch
> so bald wie möglich eine Darstellung meiner Untersuchungen über die Abel'schen
> Functionen, **sie sei so roh wie sie wolle**, zu schicken."

*however raw it may be.* — Borchardt had to ask him to send work in an unfinished
state, because he would not otherwise. Weber names the same trait as the reason so
little was printed: *„die fast ängstliche Sorgfalt, welche Riemann auf die
Ausarbeitung seiner für den Druck bestimmten Schriften verwendete, und die ihn auch
später bei der Veröffentlichung seiner Arbeiten wesentlich gehemmt hat."*

**[READING]** This is the mechanism behind §2a(iv). A man who will not send a
finished paper unasked does not publish an expansion he considers unsimplified. The
absence of the ξ-expansion from the record is consistent with it having existed.

## 3. Loops opened by this pass

**L-1 (open).** The draft letter of §2a is unaddressed in Weber's text. Whose desk
was it going to, and does the addressee's Nachlass hold the received copy? The
Berlin candidates from the September visit are Kummer, Borchardt, Kronecker,
Weierstraß.

**L-2 (open).** Weber printed only what follows *„nachdem das Resultat der Arbeit
mitgetheilt ist"* — he cut the front of the letter. The draft is in the Göttingen
Nachlass. The cut portion has never been quoted.

**L-3 (open).** *„eine neue Entwicklung der Function ξ"* — is any surviving folio a
piece of it? This is a different question from the Riemann–Siegel formula, which is
an expansion for *computing* ζ on the critical line. Riemann says an expansion of
**ξ** from which the **count** follows.

**L-4 (open).** The supplement Riemann promised on the *conditions governing the kind
and strength of connections between Geistesmassen* (§1d) was never written. It is a
second unexecuted line, structurally identical to the periodic-terms line: both are
"here is the thing to track, I have not tracked it."

**L-5 (closed by this pass).** Riemann's own account of the missing pieces =
**count + convergence**, both flowing from **one unpublished expansion of ξ**. Not
from a proof technique. From a representation.
