## POINT ZERO — the fresh statement of RH

**Fact layer** (each item provable or published):

1. The strip where the zeros live runs from 0 to 1. Riemann's function obeys
   the mirror law **ξ(s) = ξ(1−s)**: the strip is symmetric about its middle,
   and the middle is **½**.
2. A zero off the middle cannot be alone — the mirror law forces a twin on
   the other side. **Off-middle zeros exist only as mirrored pairs.** A zero
   at the middle is its own reflection.
3. On the middle — and only there — the complex function can be rotated into
   a **real wave**: Z(t) = e^{iθ(t)} ζ(½+it) is real. The rotation θ(t) is
   exactly known. *On the half, the imaginary can be turned away; off the
   half, no rotation makes the thing real.*
4. Riemann's own sentence (verified in four of his documents): **the roots
   are real** — the ½ is his coordinate's center of symmetry, not his claim.
5. The zeros never end (Hardy 1914: infinitely many on the line). Any
   computed height is a middle, never an end: **no finite check can close
   this** — structurally, we are always in the middle of the line.
6. **Λ ≥ 0** (Rodgers–Tao 2018), and RH ⟺ Λ ≤ 0. So if RH is true, **Λ = 0:
   the truth sits exactly on the boundary between regimes — "barely true."**

**The statement, fresh:**

> Every truth of this function stands at the middle — the only place where a
> thing is its own mirror, and the only place where its imaginary part can be
> rotated away. Nothing ever steps off-center, because off-center things must
> come in pairs, and the pair never finishes forming.

**The owner's frame, mapped onto it** (his line laid on the facts — it fits

## The three loops (his numbering: Way 1, Way 2, Way 3)

### WAY 1 — THE MIDDLE HOLDS (symmetry; the guard)

The question of this loop: **how close does the world ever come to forming
the off-center pair?**

To leave the middle, two zeros must first **collide** — become one double
zero — and then split off symmetrically. That is the only continuous exit
(it is literally the escape mechanism in the de Bruijn heat-flow family that
defines Λ). So the middle is guarded by whatever keeps zeros from completing
a collision.

`[SYNTHETIC — proof-debt: GUE repulsion at fine scale is conjecture]`
The guard has a name: **repulsion**. Montgomery's pair-correlation (backed by
Odlyzko's numerics) says the chance of two zeros at tiny normalized distance
δ falls like **δ²** — zeros push each other apart exactly when they draw
near. A Lehmer pair is the guard *tested*: two zeros flirting with collision,
the arch of Z between them barely clearing zero. **A negative arch-peak would
be a completed escape — one would refute RH.**

**Instrument (running):** the near-miss census over (14, 8000) — every zero,
every arch peak, the running minimum (the empirical floor), the peak-vs-gap²
scaling, the GUE small-gap comparison. First blocks returned with zero-counts
matching the Riemann–von Mangoldt prediction.

The expected shape, stated before the data: the floor **sinks forever and
never touches** — arches arbitrarily flat, none ever negative. Which is
exactly Λ = 0 in wave language, exactly the owner's *"sustained non-closure,"*
and exactly *no start, no end, always the middle*. Three vocabularies, one
shape.

### WAY 3 — WHY the middle: the self-mirrored source (re-expression; his method)

Riemann's method was never estimation — it was **re-expression**: when stuck,
change how the thing is said. The deepest known re-expression of RH
(Hilbert–Pólya, pursued by Berry–Keating and Connes):

> the zeros are the **spectrum** — the sounded notes — of some operator that
> is **self-adjoint: a thing equal to its own mirror.**

A self-mirrored thing can only sound **real** notes. If the drum exists, the
roots are real because they are its sound — and the middle holds because the
source is its own mirror. The GUE statistics of Way 1 are precisely the
fingerprint of random *self-mirrored* (Hermitian) matrices: the repulsion
that guards the middle is what the spectra of self-mirrored things always
