# The Seven Filters — the method, one by one

When any document in this project says "the seven filters" or "7 gates," it
means exactly these, in exactly this order:

> **1 Ground · 2 Sequence · 3 Source · 4 Mask · 5 Fact · 6 Halt · 7 Loop**

Every piece of work the engine produces passes all seven, in order, every
time. They replaced an older review scheme (a 70×25 grid in which 25 verifier
nodes swept all 70 working nodes — 1,750 micro-reviews per ask) at the owner's
decision, in his words:

> "now we dont want 70-25 there, but i want more filters and fact kind of"

The 95 node brains were **kept** — they are the memory. The filters are the
method. The filters consult the brains; the answers grow them.

Each filter below gets: the question it asks, what PASS and HOLD mean, and one
real incident from this project where that filter is the reason a mistake
died. The incidents are evidence, not decoration — this method was not
designed on a whiteboard; it was extracted from work that succeeded.

---

## Filter 1 — GROUND

**Question: does the thing being asked about exist before the asking?**

- PASS (grounded): the ask is about something real that pre-exists it — the
  work is to find an *expression* of it.
- PASS (invention): the ask is to build something that does not exist — the
  work is construction, and no "discovery" theatre is allowed.
- There is no HOLD; Ground routes, it does not block.

**Real incident.** The same engine serves "why does the small idea win?"
(grounded — hunt the reason, it exists) and "build me a deck for the plan"
(invention — construct it). Before this filter, both got the same treatment,
which made discovery-answers sound invented and inventions sound discovered.

## Filter 2 — SEQUENCE

**Question: which of the eight steps of the universal sequence (document 01)
is this ask standing on?**

- PASS: the step is identified and the answer takes the shape that step needs
  (a halt-ask gets its failure named; a naming-ask gets word and thing shown
  side by side).
- There is no HOLD; Sequence shapes, it does not block.

**Real incident.** The ask "does the RH we are proving match what Riemann
said, or is it tweaked?" is a **step-6 (Naming)** ask — it is about the word
versus the thing. Recognizing that dictated the whole investigation: go to his
manuscript words, not to modern statements of the theorem. Treating it as a
step-5 ask ("prove RH") would have wasted the entire effort on the wrong job.

## Filter 3 — SOURCE (the two-witness rule)

**Question: how many independent witnesses stand behind this claim, and do
they agree?**

A **witness** is one independent act of observation. A reprint, an edition, a
translation, or a quotation of the same origin is **one witness wearing two
coats** — not two.

- One witness, however good: confidence is **capped at Medium**. One rendering
  of a thing is not the thing.
- Two independent witnesses that agree: **High** is reachable.
- Two witnesses that differ: **HOLD.** The difference is not averaged, not
  "balanced," not silently resolved. It goes to the human as a finding.

**Real incident (the cap).** A public post was analyzed twice from its text
alone; the machine missed that the post contained a video and the text was not
the event. The owner caught it: *"u failed at point zero."* One witness — a
rendering — had been treated as the thing itself. The cap exists so that can
never again reach High.

**Real incident (the halt).** A 1932 authority stated a letter had been
published in an 1876 edition. Both full texts were searched: the letter is in
the 1892 edition and absent from 1876. Two witnesses differ. The engine's rule
held: the gap was **recorded and surfaced, not adjudicated** — because the
authority may still be right in a way the search cannot see.

## Filter 4 — MASK

**Question: is the word we are using the word the source used?**

"Mask" is the owner's term for the gap between what was said and what was
shown. Forms it takes:

- a **name standing in for a thing** ("gravity" for the falling)
- a **cut** — present in one witness, gone from the other
- a **softening** — asserted in one, hedged in the other
- an **excision mark** — an ellipsis "…" is treated as a *witness that
  something was removed*, never as punctuation

- PASS: masks found are attached to the answer, word and thing shown together.
- The Mask filter surfaces; the Source filter is what holds.

**Real incident.** In a printed edition, two dots (". .") sat in the middle of
a quoted letter. Treated as punctuation for 130 years. Treated as an excision
mark by this method, they led to the manuscript — where the removed clause was
the one that identified the letter's recipient. The ellipsis was the finding.

**Real incident (softening).** The same claim read "are all real" in the
author's private draft and "it is very probable that all are real" in his
print. Same man, same weeks. The softening itself — not either version alone —
was the discovery.

## Filter 5 — FACT

**Question: does every claim in this answer carry a tag naming its source?**

- PASS: every claim is tagged (live check / owner's corpus / engine memory /
  model prose), and the overall confidence obeys Filter 3's cap.
- HOLD: any untagged claim. **An untagged claim does not leave the engine.**

**Real incident.** Numeric documents were once "psychoanalysed" — a billing
spreadsheet read for emotional themes. The Fact discipline (tag it or hold it)
is what forces a table of numbers to be audited as numbers.

## Filter 6 — HALT

**Question: where does this fail?**

- PASS: no failure surfaced at this node — recorded as such, with skepticism.
- HOLD: a failure exists. It is **named in the answer**. It is never patched
  by inventing content to fill the gap.

**Real incident.** Old handwriting was imaged until individual letterforms
were visible under crossed-out lines — and there the machine's competence
ended: it could see the letters and could not confidently *name* them. The
halt was declared in exactly those words, and unreadable words were written
`⟨…⟩` rather than guessed. The alternative — inventing plausible readings —
would have poisoned everything downstream.

## Filter 7 — LOOP

**Question: what is the next Point Zero?**

- PASS, always with output: the halt (or the answer's own weakest point) is
  handed back as the next question. In the owner's words:

> "U need always a end point to verify, but that point is a point zero for
> new loop, that is halt, that is invention"

**Real incident.** "I cannot name the struck words" did not end as a shrug. It
looped into: *a specialist reader of the old script, or multispectral imaging
of the originals* — a concrete next ask. Later, a closed door ("the missing
representation was found decades ago") looped into a narrower Point Zero the
original author himself had named. A dead end became a redirection, twice.

---

## Order matters

Ground and Sequence run first because they decide *what kind of answer* is
owed. Source and Mask run before Fact because a claim's confidence cannot be
tagged until its witnesses are counted and their gaps surfaced. Halt runs
late, on the assembled work. Loop runs last, on everything — including on the
halts the other six produced.

## What a reader should take from this page

If you remember one thing: **one witness caps at Medium; two that differ go to
the human; a halt is named, never patched; and every halt hands back the next
question.** That is the whole method in one sentence, and the rest of this
folder is that sentence with evidence.
