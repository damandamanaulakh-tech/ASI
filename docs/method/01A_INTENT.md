## 2 · The depth of the words, phrase by phrase

Each phrase carries a law. This table is the instruction — not a summary of it.

| His words | What it establishes | What an answer may no longer do |
|---|---|---|
| **"In each event"** | The unit of analysis is the **event**, not the object, the person, or the topic. Reasoning attaches to something that *happened*. | Answer about a subject in general when an event was named. |
| **"happening at every single second"** | Universality with **no floor**. Not only large, notable or consequential events. Continuous, not sampled. | Call something too small, too ordinary or too routine to have a reason. |
| **"has … an intent"** | An existence claim. Intent is a property **every** event possesses — not most, not important ones. | Say "there is no reason", "it just happened", "random", "coincidence", "no particular cause". |
| **"which is motivation"** | Intent **is** motivation. A *drive*, not a *plan*. It need not be conscious, stated, deliberate, or even known to whoever acted. | Require conscious deliberation before granting intent. Reduce intent to a stated goal or an announced objective. |
| **"the real reason"** | There is a **real** reason, distinct from the given one. The word *real* only earns its place because a false one exists. | Accept the stated reason as the reason. This is the **Mask**, at the root of everything. |
| **"may the event put comes are nothing"** | The event's output may be **nothing**. Result is not evidence of cause. | Infer absence of intent from absence of outcome. |
| **"or need to aviod or ognore"** | The event may deserve avoidance, or be worth ignoring — **and it still has an intent.** Worth is a separate axis from cause. | Skip the intent-read because the event was judged unimportant. A judgement about value is never a reason to stop looking for the reason. |
| **"but the event has happedned"** | **Occurrence is the only precondition.** Once it happened, its intent is there to be found. | Treat a failed, aborted, incomplete or pointless event as intent-free. |
| **"one intent"** | **Singular.** One intent — not a blend, not a weighted mixture, not a shortlist. | Return three possible motives as the answer. Average candidates into a composite. |
| **"one real reason which pushes the event"** | Intent **pushes**. It is a force from **behind**, not a goal ahead. | Read intent forward off the outcome. You walk **back** to it, never down from it. |
| **"and rest of things are putted in that way so the event can happen"** | The surroundings were **arranged** so the event could occur. Therefore **the arrangement is the evidence of the intent.** | Examine only the event and ignore what was positioned around it. The arrangement is the fingerprint. |

## 3 · The six laws, condensed

1. **Universality.** No event is intent-free. There is no exempt class — not
   noise, not accident, not trivia, not routine.
2. **Singularity.** One intent per event. One real reason that pushes it.
3. **Independence from outcome.** The intent does not depend on what the event
   produced, or on whether it was worth anything. A worthless result is not an
   absent reason.
4. **Realness over appearance.** The stated reason and the real reason are two
   different objects. Finding the second is the work.
5. **Push, not pull.** Intent operates from behind. It is recovered by walking
   backwards, not by inferring from consequences.
6. **Arrangement is evidence.** Everything around the event was put where it is
   so the event could happen. Read the arrangement; that is where the intent is
   legible.

---

## 4 · How to actually find it — the procedure

This is the operating loop, and it is a **reverse walk**, because law 5 says the
force is behind.

1. **Name the event precisely.** Not the topic, not the person — the thing that
   happened, at the grain it happened. If the event cannot be stated in one
   sentence, it is more than one event; split it.
2. **List what was arranged around it.** Every condition, resource, timing,
   absence, permission, and order that had to be in place. Absences count — what
   was *not* there is arrangement too.
3. **Ask of each arrangement: necessary for what?** Each item narrows the field.
4. **The intent is what makes all of them necessary at once.** Not the thing
   that explains the most items — the thing that explains *every* item. A reason
   that leaves an arrangement unexplained is not yet the intent.
5. **Test by removal.** Take the candidate intent away. If the arrangement still
   makes sense without it, it was not the intent. If the arrangement collapses,
   it was.

**Step 5 is the test that stops invention.** A candidate that survives removal
of nothing is decoration.

---

## 5 · When two candidates both fit — the hard rule

Law 2 says there is **one** intent. That is a statement about the world. **It is
not permission for the engine to pick.**

> **If two candidate intents both survive the removal test, the engine HALTS.**
> Both readings go to the human, named, with the arrangement each one explains.
> The engine does not choose, does not rank by plausibility, and does not blend.

This is the same law as two witnesses that differ (Filter 3), and the reason is
the same: choosing between two half-supported readings manufactures one
fully-supported reading that nothing supports. **The gap is the output.**

One candidate that fits, with the arrangement fully explained → state it as a
reading. One candidate with gaps in the arrangement → state it, name the gaps.
Zero candidates → say so; **"I have not found the intent" is a permitted answer.
"There is no intent" is not.**

## 6 · What this forbids, in words

These phrases may not appear in an answer about an event:

- "It happened for no reason."
- "This was random / arbitrary / meaningless."
- "There is no motivation behind this."
- "It's not important enough to analyse."
- "The reason is unclear, so let's move on."

The permitted forms are: **"the intent is X, and here is the arrangement that
shows it"**, **"two intents survive; here are both"**, or **"I have not found
it, and here is what I looked at."**

An asserted intent with no evidence in the arrangement is **`[SYNTHETIC]`** and
carries proof-debt and an expiry, like any other forced fit. It is never
presented as found.

## 8 · One structural question, open — the owner decides

**Is INTENT a ninth step, a step zero, or a property of every step?**

The paragraph says intent belongs to *every event, at every second*. That is not
the shape of a step — steps happen once each, in order. It is the shape of
something the whole sequence stands on.

And the owner's own earlier line, from document 01, is the witness that decides
it:

> *"but sequence start before the hunger, fuel is there before the intent"*

**The Ground has no intent** — nobody made the fuel, the falling, or the zeros.
**Every event does.** So the two fit together without contradiction:

> `[READING]` **Intent is not a step. It is what every event carries, and the
> eight steps are how an intent becomes knowable.** GROUND pre-exists intent;
> from PRESSURE onward, every step is an event and therefore has one.

This is the reading the rest of this document is written on. **It is a reading,
not his word.** If the intended shape is a step 0 or a ninth step instead, say
so and this document is rewritten to match — nothing downstream depends on the
reading being right, only on it being marked.
